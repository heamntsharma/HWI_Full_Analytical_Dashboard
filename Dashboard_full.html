Chapters 1–2 · Introduction & Literature Review

§1.5 – 1.6

Problem statement & rationale

The gap between rapidly advancing ML methods and their underutilisation in operational Indian heatwave prediction

Narrow geographic scope

Existing studies tend to focus on single states or meteorological subdivisions.

Limited predictor sets

MODIS LST/NDVI rarely combined with conventional met. observations.

Narrow algorithm comparison

A narrow range of algorithms applied without systematic comparative evaluation.

No CMIP6 SSP translation

Translating ML skill into future SSP-scenario projections remains largely unexplored for India.

Three pillars of rationale (§1.6): public health, scientific, policy
Why this matters

~45% of the national workforce works in outdoor agriculture, construction, or informal labour with little capacity to avoid peak heat exposure, and the 2015 heatwave caused >2,500 recorded deaths. Better lead time on prediction supports earlier activation of Heat Action Plans.

§1.12 & §2.10

Research gap analysis

Five gaps identified through the literature review, and a structured study-by-study comparison

Gap 1 — Regional

ML heatwave prediction is well developed for Europe, North America, East Asia; comparable South Asia / India work remains limited.

Gap 2 — Data integration

Studies typically use meteorological or remote-sensing predictors, rarely both in one framework.

Gap 3 — Algorithmic rigor

Rigorous systematic comparison across traditional ML and deep learning in one framework is lacking for India.

Gap 4 — Future-scenario

Explicit CMIP6 integration into ML heatwave frameworks remains understudied.

Gap 5 — Interpretability

SHAP / permutation importance rarely applied comprehensively at national scale for India.

§1.8 Research Hypotheses — six objectives, each paired with a null (H₀) / alternative (H₁) hypothesis

All six objective–hypothesis pairs

Scientific interpretation

Each objective is tested through a classical null-hypothesis significance testing (NHST) pair. The six pairs form a logical chain: O1/H1 must hold before O2/H2 is meaningful; O3/H3 (generalisation) gates whether O4/H4 (future projections) can be trusted; O5/H5 tests whether fusing historical + future data adds robustness; O6/H6 asks whether specific climatic variables — not random variation — drive heatwave intensity.

PHASE 1 / 10

Raw data collection

Objective 1: Historical records & climatic variables, 2000–2025

IMD gridded meteorologicalsource ↗

  • Relative Humidity, Wind Speed

MODIS satellitesource ↗

  • LST (MOD11A1), NDVI (MOD13A1)

  • PM2.5

Years: 2000–2025

Scientific interpretation

Four independent observation systems — station data (IMD), satellite remote sensing (MODIS), global reanalysis (ERA5), regulatory air-quality monitoring (CPCB) — triangulate the heat signal from complementary vantage points, mitigating single-instrument bias. HWF and HWI are derived directly from IMD's operational temperature-based heatwave criteria.

PHASE 4 / 10

Temporal train / test split

Chronological split to ensure robust model validation and avoid data leakage

Train — 2000–2017

  • ~5,569 rows (70%)

Test — 2018–2025

  • ~2,387 rows (30%)
Scientific interpretation

A chronological — not random — split is essential given strong autocorrelation in climate data. Same principle enforced inside TimeSeriesSplit CV (Phase 5) and Leave-One-Region-Out validation (Phase 6).

PHASE 5 / 10

Machine learning model development & training

Objective 3: Develop and evaluate leakage-free predictive models for HWI

Candidates

  • Linear, Ridge, Random Forest
  • Gradient Boosting ★ primary
  • SVR (RBF), MLP Regressor

Validation strategy

  • TimeSeriesSplit, k=5, no shuffle
  • GridSearchCV tuning

Leakage-free set

  • HSI_lag1, HSI_roll3 removed
Scientific interpretation

Six model families benchmarked under identical leakage-free, time-aware CV — isolating the comparison to model capacity. Removing HSI_lag1/HSI_roll3 prevents the model from "cheating" via nowcasting.

PHASE 6 / 10

Model validation & performance evaluation

Objective 4: Assess model robustness, accuracy, and generalisability

Temporal 5-fold CV

Mean R² 0.911 ± 0.013

Spatial LORO-CV

Mean R² 0.875 ± 0.022

Test set (2018–2025)

R² 0.982 · RMSE 0.162 · MAE 0.123 · TSS 0.951
Scientific interpretation

Spatial LORO-CV (0.875) is the more demanding test — it withholds an entire region, and its modestly lower score vs. temporal CV (0.911) is expected: it quantifies genuine geographic transferability rather than memorised local patterns.

PHASE 8 / 10

Historical EDA & trend analysis

Scientific interpretation

Non-parametric methods are robust to the non-normal, outlier-prone distributions typical of climate extremes — a single anomalous year should not dominate the trend the way it can in an OLS fit.

Scientific interpretation

Bootstrap resampling across districts (B=1,000) propagates spatial sampling uncertainty into a 90% CI. Divergence between SSP1-2.6 (mitigation) and SSP5-8.5 (high emissions) by end-century is the expected signature of a well-behaved climate-sensitive model.
